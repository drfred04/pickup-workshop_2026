Part 1 · Find the zero-force members

Zero-force members carry no load, so finding them first lets you eliminate them from the rest of the analysis. Tap a member in the drawing (or use the chips below it) to mark it as a zero-force member. For each one you mark, pick the joint whose equilibrium proves the member carries no force. Treat the members as weightless, so the only loads are the ones shown.

Truss

    Hint: the two inspection rules

    Rule 1 (two members): If an unloaded joint connects only two members that are not collinear, both members are zero-force.

    Rule 2 (three members, two collinear): If an unloaded joint connects three members and two of them are collinear, the third (non-collinear) member is zero-force.

    "Unloaded" means no applied load and no support reaction acts at the joint.

            Part 5 · Solve for the three member forces

            Find each cut member's force from your FBD, in any order. For each one, pick an equation that contains that force as its only unknown. Forces you have already found count as known. Write the equation with +x right, +y up and counterclockwise moments positive (clockwise-positive is also accepted). Use the reaction labels or their values, and the member labels or values you have already found. When you report each force, give a positive magnitude and say whether the member is in tension or compression; a negative result from your equation means the actual sense is opposite to the one you assumed.

            Part 5 unlocks when your FBD in Part 4 passes.
            Hint: choosing equations

            To isolate one cut member, take moments about the point where the lines of the other two cut members meet. Their moment arms are zero there. If the other two members are parallel, they never meet: sum forces perpendicular to them instead (for two horizontal chords, use ΣFy).
