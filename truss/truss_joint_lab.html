Part 1 · Find the zero-force members

Tap a member in the drawing (or use the chips below it) to mark it as a zero-force member. For each one you mark, pick the joint whose equilibrium proves the member carries no force. Treat the members as weightless, so the only loads are the ones shown.

Truss

    Hint: the two inspection rules

    Rule 1 (two members): If an unloaded joint connects only two members that are not collinear, both members are zero-force.

    Rule 2 (three members, two collinear): If an unloaded joint connects three members and two of them are collinear, the third (non-collinear) member is zero-force.

    "Unloaded" means no applied load and no support reaction acts at the joint.

    Part 2 · Draw a free-body diagram for each joint

        Part 3 · Write the equilibrium equations for each joint

        For each joint, write ΣFx = 0 and ΣFy = 0 so they match your free-body diagram from Part 2. Take +x to the right and +y upward. A force drawn in the negative direction gets a minus sign, and that includes member forces you drew as compression. Use the sign of the arrow you drew, whatever value the force turns out to have.

        How to type your equations

        Use your FBD labels: F_AB (or FAB), . Write applied loads as plain numbers in kN, with no units. Example: F_BC - F_AB = 0.

        The “= 0” is optional, and you may move terms to the right-hand side. Zero-force members can be included or left out, except at a joint where they are the only forces in that direction. There, the equation is what proves them zero.

              Hint: choosing the moment point

              Two of the three unknown reactions act at . Their moments about are zero, so ΣM = 0 contains only one unknown. Any other point works too, including one off the truss: the moment equation will then contain more than one reaction, and you solve it together with ΣFx and ΣFy.

              5c · Solve

              Complete 5b first.

              Solve your three equations, then report each reaction as a positive magnitude in kN and the direction it actually acts. If an equation gives a negative value, the reaction acts opposite to the arrow you drew. You can type a number or an arithmetic expression such as 144/12.

                Part 6 · Solve for the member forces

                With the reactions known, work through the truss joint by joint, starting at a joint with no more than two unknowns. Report each member's force the way engineers do: a positive magnitude in kN and its sense, tension or compression (or zero-force). Your equations use the senses you assumed on your FBDs, so a member you guessed wrong will come out negative. That sign tells you the actual sense; leave it out of the reported magnitude.
