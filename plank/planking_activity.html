Now the person holds the same plank position on a straight inclined board, feet elevated toward the upper end. Both contact points sit directly on the board, so the normal forces are perpendicular to the board and friction acts along the board. It is easiest to use axes aligned with the board: x′ along the board (up-slope positive) and y′ perpendicular to the board. Resolve the weight into components along these axes: W∥ = W·sin(theta) (down-slope) and W⊥ = W·cos(theta) (into the board).

1. Build the free-body diagram

Four real forces act on the isolated body: the weight at the CG, a normal force and a friction force at the feet, and a normal force and a friction force at the hands. Choose each direction. (Static equilibrium of a rigid body with two contact points only determines the combined friction demand, not how it individually splits between the two points. Any choice of friction directions is accepted as long as, together, they can keep the body from sliding down the slope. You will solve for the total friction magnitude below.)
